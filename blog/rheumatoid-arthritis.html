Rheumatoid Arthritis

By Dr. Sai Lakshmikanth Bharathi, MD · August 5, 2026

Why Early Diagnosis and Consistent Treatment Matter More Than You Think
Dr. Sai Lakshmikanth Bharathi, MD General Medicine | Founder, C-MEDIS

A few weeks ago, a young woman in her late twenties walked into my clinic with pain and stiffness in multiple joints — hands, wrists, knees — worse in the mornings, lasting over an hour before she could move freely. Blood work and clinical examination confirmed rheumatoid arthritis (RA).

  • We started her on methotrexate along with a short course of steroids to control the acute inflammation. Within weeks, she was noticeably better — less pain, less stiffness, back to her routine.
  • Then she paused treatment to try a native/alternative remedy, hoping for a "natural cure." Months later, she returned — joints more swollen, stiffness worse, function significantly reduced.
  • We restarted the same treatment she'd left, and she is recovering well again. But the months lost in between were months her joints didn't get back.
  • This story is far more common than people realize, and it's exactly why I wanted to write this.

Why Early Recognition Matters

  • Rheumatoid arthritis is not "just joint pain." It's a chronic autoimmune condition where the body's immune system attacks the lining of its own joints.
  • Left untreated or inadequately treated, it doesn't stay still — it progressively erodes cartilage and bone, leading to irreversible joint damage, deformity, and disability, often within the first two years of disease onset.

The signs to watch for:

  • Joint pain and swelling in more than one joint, usually symmetric (both hands, both wrists, both knees)
  • Morning stiffness lasting more than 30–60 minutes
  • Fatigue, low-grade fever, or unexplained weight loss alongside joint symptoms
  • Symptoms persisting more than 6 weeks
  • The window for early, effective intervention — often called the "window of opportunity" — is critical.
  • The earlier DMARDs (Disease-Modifying Anti-Rheumatic Drugs) like methotrexate are started, the better the long-term joint outcomes and the lower the risk of permanent damage.

The Danger of Interrupting Treatment

  • RA is a marathon, not a sprint. It requires sustained, monitored treatment — not a course you stop the moment you feel better, and not something to abandon for unproven alternative remedies without medical supervision.
  • Stopping DMARDs mid-treatment allows disease activity to flare back, often more aggressively, and can undo months of progress. If you or a loved one wants to explore complementary approaches, please discuss it with your treating physician first — don't substitute it for evidence-based therapy.

A Special Note for Women in the Reproductive Age Group

  • RA disproportionately affects women, many in their reproductive years, which makes medication counseling especially important:
  • Methotrexate is teratogenic — it can cause serious birth defects and is not safe during pregnancy or while trying to conceive. Effective contraception is essential while on this medication, for both women and men (partners) planning to conceive.
  • If pregnancy is planned, methotrexate should be stopped at least 3 months in advance (in consultation with your doctor), and treatment transitioned to pregnancy-safer alternatives such as hydroxychloroquine or sulfasalazine, under close specialist supervision.
  • Folic acid supplementation is routinely given alongside methotrexate to reduce side effects — this is separate from pregnancy planning and should continue as advised.
  • Uncontrolled RA itself carries risks in pregnancy, so the goal is never simply to stop medication — it's to plan the transition safely with your doctor well in advance.
  • Breastfeeding compatibility also differs by drug, so this should always be individualized — please don't self-adjust medications.

The Takeaway

  • Rheumatoid arthritis is very manageable when caught early and treated consistently. The biggest risks come from delayed diagnosis, treatment gaps, and self-directed changes to medication — especially around pregnancy planning.
  • If you or someone you know has persistent joint pain and stiffness, please don't wait it out. Early consultation with a physician or rheumatologist can make the difference between a full, active life and irreversible joint damage.

This post is for general awareness and does not replace individualized medical advice. Please consult your treating doctor for decisions about your specific condition.
